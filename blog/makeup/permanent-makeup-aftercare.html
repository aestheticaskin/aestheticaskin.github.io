Permanent Makeup Aftercare — The Complete Healing Guide

The procedure takes 2 hours. The result lasts 2 years. But what happens in the 14 days between determines which of those two outcomes actually shows up.

Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg

MD Dermatology · Aesthetica Skin Clinic, Punawale, Pune

She sent me a message on day five. It was short, but the urgency was unmistakable.

"My brows look terrible. They're patchy and one side is darker than the other and parts of it look like it's coming off. I think something went wrong."

Nothing had gone wrong. Day five is, almost universally, the ugliest day of the permanent makeup healing process. The skin is actively shedding. The pigment appears to lighten, sometimes patchy and uneven, sometimes dramatically different from what you saw in the mirror leaving the clinic. It is alarming if you have not been warned. It is entirely predictable if you have.

I called her back and explained what was happening: the outer skin layer healing over the pigment, the superficial colour lifting away as skin cells shed, the deeper pigment waiting underneath — untouched, intact, settling into its final tone. "Come in at week six," I told her. "You will not recognise how worried you are right now."

She sent another message six weeks later. One photograph. No words needed.

This is the guide I wish every permanent makeup client read before their appointment — not after day five.

"I tell every client: the procedure is my job. The healing is yours. I can do perfect work and still produce a mediocre result if the 14 days after are not respected."

— Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg, MD Dermatology

What Is Actually Happening Under Your Skin

Permanent makeup deposits pigment into the upper dermis — just below the epidermis (the outer skin layer). During healing, the epidermis closes over the pigment, and a thin layer of skin regenerates on top. This is why the colour appears to fade and lighten in the first 10–14 days — you are seeing the skin healing over the pigment, not the pigment disappearing.

As this surface layer settles and becomes transparent, the true healed colour reveals itself. The final result — the one that is yours for 1–3 years — is only fully visible at 4–6 weeks. Everything before that is the process, not the result.

Understanding this one thing eliminates 90% of the anxiety that accompanies the healing phase.

Day-by-Day: What to Expect

Days 1–3Most Critical

The treated area appears its darkest and most intense — 30–50% darker than the final result. Mild swelling and redness are normal. The skin feels slightly tight. Keep the area completely dry, clean, and protected. Apply provided healing ointment with a clean cotton bud 2–3 times daily — a thin layer, not a thick coat. Do not touch with unwashed hands.

Days 4–7The Ugly Phase

The skin begins to peel and flake. For brows, this looks like tiny flakes lifting at the edges of the strokes. For lips, the colour visibly lightens as the outer pigment layer sheds. For eyeliner, a thin crust may form along the lash line. Do not pick, peel, or rub any flaking skin. This is the most common aftercare mistake and it pulls pigment out prematurely, creating permanent patchiness. Let the skin shed entirely on its own timeline.

Days 8–14Settling

Surface healing is largely complete. The colour has lightened significantly from the initial intensity — possibly more than you expected. This is normal. The skin is still healing at the dermal level. Pigment is settling into its long-term position. Continue avoiding active skincare, sun, and makeup on the treated area.

Weeks 3–4Emerging

The true colour begins to emerge as the skin fully closes over the pigment and becomes transparent. You may notice the colour looks slightly faded or patchy — this is what the touch-up session addresses. The final result is still not yet visible.

Week 6: Touch-UpFinal Step

The included touch-up appointment at 4–6 weeks is where the final result is locked in. Dr. Prachi corrects any areas that healed lighter, perfects symmetry, deepens colour where needed, and completes the process. This session is not optional — it is the second half of the procedure. Skipping it is the single most common reason for suboptimal permanent makeup outcomes.

The Universal Dos and Don'ts — All Procedures

check_circle Do

  • Apply healing ointment as directed — thin layers, clean applicator
  • Sleep on a clean pillowcase for 2 weeks
  • Keep the area dry and protected from water for 10 days
  • Attend the 4–6 week touch-up without exception
  • Apply SPF 50+ daily after the healing period — especially in Pune's UV climate
  • Let flaking skin shed completely on its own

cancel Do Not

  • Pick, peel, scratch, or rub any flaking skin — ever
  • Get the treated area wet for 10 days (washing, swimming, rain)
  • Exercise or sweat heavily for 10–14 days
  • Apply makeup over healing brows or lips
  • Use retinoids, AHAs, or active skincare near the treated area for 4 weeks
  • Expose the area to direct sun, saunas, or steam for 4 weeks

Procedure-Specific Aftercare

Microblading & Powder Brows

  • Keep brows completely dry for 10 days — wash around them carefully with a damp cloth
  • Do not apply eyebrow makeup, pencil, or gel during healing
  • Avoid washing hair in a way that runs water over the brow area — lean back or use a shower cap
  • Do not apply retinol or AHA products anywhere near the brow area for 4 weeks
  • Wear a hat outdoors to protect from UV during healing and after

Lip Blush

  • Apply provided lip balm or healing ointment every 2–3 hours for the first 7 days
  • Eat soft, room-temperature foods — avoid very hot, spicy, or salty food for 5 days
  • Drink with a straw for the first 5 days to minimise contact with liquids on the healing area
  • Do not apply lip products, lipstick, or gloss during the healing period
  • If you have a history of cold sores, continue prescribed antiviral medication for 3–5 days post-treatment

Permanent Eyeliner

  • Do not wear contact lenses for at least 3–5 days — use glasses instead
  • Apply provided ointment gently to the lash line using a clean cotton bud
  • Do not apply eye makeup, mascara, or eye shadow during healing
  • Avoid rubbing your eyes — even gently — for 10 days
  • Keep shower water away from the eye area; wash your face with a cloth around the eyes

The day 5 rule: If your permanent makeup looks alarming at day 4, 5, or 6 — it is healing correctly. The patchy, faded, flaking phase is not a sign something went wrong. It is the sign that something is going exactly right. Trust the process. Judge the result at week six — never before.

When to Actually Be Concerned

Most "problems" in the first two weeks are the normal healing process — and should be left alone. But there are genuine warning signs that warrant contacting your practitioner:

  • Significant swelling that is increasing rather than decreasing after day 3
  • Warmth, spreading redness, or yellow discharge — signs of possible infection
  • Severe itching that is intensifying (mild itching during healing is normal)
  • Any allergic reaction symptoms — hives, rash spreading beyond the treated area

These are uncommon when procedures are performed in a properly sterile clinical environment — but they are not impossible. Contact Aesthetica immediately if you notice any of these. Do not wait and hope they resolve.

Back to Her Message at Day Five

She had not picked at the flaking skin — which was the most important thing. She had applied her healing ointment. She had kept the area dry. She had, despite the panic, followed the aftercare she had been given.

Which is why at week six, the touch-up session took less than twenty minutes. There was barely anything to correct. The brows had healed evenly, the colour was settled, the strokes were clean.

"I thought it was ruined," she said, studying herself in the consultation mirror.

"It was always fine," I told her. "You just had to give it time to show you."

That is the entire story of permanent makeup aftercare. Not complicated. Not restrictive forever. Just two weeks of care, one touch-up appointment — and then two years of waking up looking effortlessly like yourself.
