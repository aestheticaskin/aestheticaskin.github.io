Laser Resurfacing vs Microneedling — Which is Right for Your Skin?

Both promise to fix scars, pigmentation and aging skin. Both are backed by real clinical evidence. So why do so many patients choose the wrong one — and spend months wondering why results are underwhelming?

Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg

MD Dermatology · Aesthetica Skin Clinic, Punawale, Pune

He was a 29-year-old architect with acne scars that had been living rent-free on his cheeks since college. He came in armed with a very specific question: "I've read everything online and I still can't figure out — laser or microneedling?"

I told him what I tell every patient who asks me this: The internet gives you a comparison. I give you a diagnosis.

Because the honest answer isn't laser or microneedling. The honest answer is: it depends on what your skin is actually doing, what it needs, and what it can safely tolerate. And for Indian skin especially, that calculation is more nuanced than most comparison articles will tell you.

So let's do it properly.

The Mechanism: What Each Treatment Actually Does

To choose correctly, you need to understand the biology — not just the before-and-after photographs.

Laser Resurfacing: Light as a Surgeon

A medical laser delivers focused light energy to the skin at a specific wavelength. Depending on the laser type, this energy is absorbed by water in the skin cells (ablative lasers like CO₂ and Er:YAG), by melanin and pigment deposits (Q-switch lasers), or by the dermal tissue beneath the surface (non-ablative and fractional lasers).

The result: controlled destruction of targeted tissue layers. Your body, recognising this as injury, launches a healing response — producing new collagen, new elastin, new cells to replace what was removed. Done correctly, you end up with skin that is smoother, tighter, and more evenly pigmented than what was there before.

The key word is controlled. The laser removes. The body replaces. The skill lies in removing exactly the right amount, at the right depth, with the right wavelength for your skin tone.

Microneedling: Needles as Architects

Microneedling takes a different route to the same destination. A device with fine sterile needles creates thousands of precise micro-channels in the skin — tiny injuries that are invisible to the naked eye. No tissue is removed. Nothing is destroyed.

Instead, the healing cascade is triggered purely through the wound response — the skin floods the area with growth factors, platelet activity, and new collagen synthesis. Combined with PRP (platelet-rich plasma) applied during the session, this process is significantly amplified.

The result: firmer, smoother, more refined skin — built from within rather than resurfaced from above.

"Laser remodels the skin by removing and replacing. Microneedling remodels it by building. Neither is universally superior — the right choice depends on what your skin needs rebuilt and how."

— Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg, MD Dermatology

The Indian Skin Equation

This is where the conversation gets important — and where generic comparison articles usually fail you.

Indian skin (Fitzpatrick III–V) carries melanocytes that are both more active and more reactive than lighter skin types. Any heat-based treatment — and laser resurfacing generates significant heat — risks triggering post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH): a darkening of the skin that can be worse than the original problem.

This is not a reason to avoid laser on Indian skin. It is a reason to be extremely precise about which laser, at what settings, on which patient. Q-switch lasers for pigmentation, fractional non-ablative lasers for texture — these can be used safely and effectively on Indian skin by an experienced dermatologist. But the margin for error is smaller than with lighter skin types.

Microneedling, by contrast, creates no heat and does not target melanin. The PIH risk is substantially lower. This is why, for many Indian patients presenting with acne scars on moderately to deeply pigmented skin, I recommend starting with microneedling — and introducing laser selectively once the skin's baseline reactivity is understood.

The risk nobody tells you about: Laser treatment on Indian skin performed by someone without experience in darker skin types is one of the fastest ways to end up with new pigmentation problems layered on top of the original ones. The technology is not the risk — the calibration is.

The Case for Combining Both

Here is something the laser vs microneedling debate often misses: the best outcomes frequently come from combining both treatments strategically.

A typical protocol for moderate acne scarring on Indian skin might look like this at Aesthetica:

  • Sessions 1–3: Microneedling with PRP to build baseline collagen and assess skin reactivity
  • Sessions 4–5: Fractional non-ablative laser to address deeper scar architecture and any residual pigmentation
  • Maintenance: Quarterly microneedling to sustain collagen levels

This staged approach maximises results while managing risk — giving the skin time to respond at each phase before escalating to more aggressive modalities.

So — Which One For You?

Let me give you the honest clinical shorthand:

  • Choose microneedling if: you have rolling or shallow acne scars, darker skin tone, minimal downtime available, or you are new to clinical skin treatments and want to start conservatively
  • Choose laser if: you have deep pitted scars, significant active pigmentation, skin laxity requiring dramatic tightening, or previous microneedling that has plateaued
  • Consider both if: you have a complex presentation — multiple scar types, both texture and pigmentation concerns, or skin that has partially responded to one treatment and needs a different mechanism to continue improving

Back to the Architect

He had rolling and boxcar scars — moderate depth, spread across both cheeks, with some residual PIH from old breakouts. His skin tone was Fitzpatrick IV.

We started with four sessions of microneedling with PRP, spaced four weeks apart. By session three, the rolling scars had softened considerably. The boxcar scars — the deeper, sharper-edged ones — were less responsive. After session four, we introduced fractional non-ablative laser for two sessions, targeting precisely those resistant areas.

The combined result was better than either treatment alone would have produced. At month seven, he sent a photograph from his firm's annual presentation. Professional headshot, boardroom setting, natural light. Clear skin. No concealer.

He didn't need a comparison article. He needed a dermatologist who understood both tools well enough to know which one to reach for, when, and in what order.

What is the difference between laser and microneedling?

Laser uses focused light energy to remove damaged skin layers and target pigmentation — working from the outside in. Microneedling creates micro-injuries with fine needles to trigger collagen production from within, without removing skin. Both improve scars and texture, but through different mechanisms suited to different concerns.

Which is better for acne scars on Indian skin?

For shallow, rolling scars on Indian skin, microneedling with PRP is often the safer starting point — effective with lower risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. For deeper, pitted scars, fractional laser provides stronger remodelling per session. A staged combination of both often produces the best long-term outcomes.

Which treatment has less downtime?

Microneedling typically involves 24–48 hours of redness. Non-ablative laser has similar recovery. Ablative laser resurfacing requires 5–7 days. For patients who cannot afford visible downtime, microneedling or non-ablative laser are better suited to their lifestyle.
