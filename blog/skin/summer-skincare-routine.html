The Complete Summer Skincare Routine for Pune's Climate

Heat, humidity, UV, dust, pollution. Pune's summer is an obstacle course for your skin. Here's how to win it — with a dermatologist-designed routine built specifically for this city.

Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg

MD Dermatology · Aesthetica Skin Clinic, Punawale, Pune

It was a Tuesday morning in April. She came in holding her phone screen towards me — a photo taken that morning in bathroom mirror light. "Look at this," she said. "I didn't have this last October."

The melasma patch across her left cheek had deepened noticeably. Her T-zone was shining by 9am. Around her nose, the pores looked larger than she remembered. And there was a breakout along her jaw that hadn't been there in the cooler months.

She was doing everything "right." Double cleansing. Vitamin C. Retinol at night. A good moisturiser. But she was doing October's routine in April. And in Pune, that is a very different situation.

Pune's summer — stretching from March through June before the monsoon finally arrives — combines high UV index, temperatures that regularly breach 38°C, rising humidity, dust, and the particulate pollution of a rapidly expanding city. Your skin is not dealing with one stressor. It is dealing with five simultaneously. The routine that kept your skin clear through December is actively working against you by May.

This is what I told her. And this is what I tell every patient who comes in with summer skin complaints: you don't need more products. You need the right ones, in the right order, calibrated for what this city actually does to your skin.

First: Understand What Pune Summer Does to Indian Skin

Before the routine, the biology. Because if you understand why your skin behaves the way it does in summer, the routine makes intuitive sense rather than feeling like a set of arbitrary rules.

Heat triggers increased sebum production — your skin's natural oil. This is why oily and combination skin types feel like they're drowning in their own face by lunchtime. The excess oil mixes with the day's pollution, dust, and sweat to create a film that blocks pores. This is the biological origin of those summer breakouts along the jawline and forehead that seem to appear from nowhere.

Meanwhile, UV intensity in Pune between 10am and 3pm from March to June is severe. And for Indian skin — Fitzpatrick types III–V — UV exposure is the single biggest trigger for melanin overproduction. That means pigmentation. The patches that appeared on the cheeks. The post-acne marks that refuse to fade. The gradual darkening that accumulates over years of inadequate sun protection.

Add humidity-driven dehydration (yes — humid air still dehydrates the skin through trans-epidermal water loss), and you have a confluence of stressors that standard skincare advice does not address.

"The biggest mistake I see is patients continuing their winter routine into summer. Heavy moisturisers, oils, rich creams — they're suffocating already-stressed summer skin."

— Dr. Prachi Gupta Garg, MD Dermatology

The Morning Routine

1
AM · Step 1

Gel Cleanser — Brief & Gentle

Switch your morning cleanser to a gel or foam formula with a low, slightly acidic pH (4.5–5.5). This removes overnight sebum and sweat without stripping the skin barrier. Avoid sulphate-heavy formulations — they over-cleanse and trigger a rebound oil surge by midday. 60 seconds maximum. Rinse thoroughly.

2
AM · Step 2

Vitamin C Serum — Your Pigmentation Shield

For Indian skin in summer, a stabilised Vitamin C serum (L-ascorbic acid, 10–15%) applied every morning is non-negotiable. It neutralises free radicals from UV exposure before they can trigger melanin production, brightens existing pigmentation, and synergises with your sunscreen to amplify UV protection. Apply to damp skin, allow 30 seconds to absorb.

3
AM · Step 3

Lightweight Hydrating Serum or Gel Moisturiser

This is the step most oily-skin patients skip in summer — and it is a mistake. Dehydrated skin overproduces oil as compensation. A water-based hyaluronic acid serum or gel moisturiser provides hydration without adding lipids. Look for formulations that also contain niacinamide — a 4–5% concentration will regulate sebum and reinforce the barrier simultaneously.

4
AM · Step 4 · MOST IMPORTANT

SPF 50+ PA+++ Sunscreen — Apply Generously, Reapply Often

This is the single most important step for Indian skin in Pune summer. Full stop. Use SPF 50 minimum, with PA+++ (UVA protection) — UVA is responsible for the deep, pigment-triggering rays that glass and clouds do not block. Apply ¼ teaspoon for the face alone — most people apply 20–30% of the amount needed for stated SPF efficacy. Reapply every 2–3 hours if outdoors. For oily skin, matte or gel sunscreens prevent that thick, greasy finish.

The Evening Routine

1
PM · Step 1

Double Cleanse — Remove Everything

In summer, a double cleanse is worthwhile even if you don't wear makeup. First, a micellar water or light cleansing oil dissolves sunscreen and pollutant residue. Follow with your gel cleanser. Sunscreen that sits on skin overnight clogs pores and contributes to the congestion that becomes breakouts. Do not skip this.

2
PM · Step 2

Niacinamide Serum — Regulate, Repair, Brighten

Niacinamide at 4–5% is the summer workhorse ingredient for Indian skin. At this concentration it regulates sebum, reduces post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, strengthens the skin barrier, and calms redness — addressing the four most common summer complaints in one step. Use every evening.

3
PM · Step 3

Retinol — With a Summer Caveat

Retinol remains one of the most evidence-backed ingredients for skin cell turnover, pigmentation, and anti-aging. But it increases photosensitivity — so in summer, reduce to 2–3 nights per week (down from nightly), use only at night, and be meticulous about sunscreen the following morning. If you are experiencing active breakouts or barrier disruption, pause retinol until skin is settled.

4
PM · Step 4

Light Night Moisturiser — Not Your Winter Cream

Swap your rich winter moisturiser for a lighter gel or fluid formulation. Summer skin does not need heavy emollients — it needs barrier support without occlusion. A gel moisturiser with ceramides and hyaluronic acid provides all the repair and hydration without the weight that contributes to congestion and milia in the heat.

The Rules That Actually Matter

  • Never skip sunscreen. Even on overcast days — up to 80% of UV penetrates cloud cover
  • Reapply sunscreen every 2–3 hours if outdoors. One morning application is not sufficient protection for a Pune summer day
  • Avoid peak UV hours — 11am to 3pm — as much as practically possible
  • Wear physical protection: wide-brimmed hat, UV-protective clothing when outdoors for extended periods
  • Hydrate internally — 8–10 glasses of water daily. Dehydrated skin is compromised skin
  • Do not over-exfoliate in summer — heat already accelerates cell turnover and barrier sensitisation. Limit to one exfoliation (chemical or physical) per week maximum

The summer treatment pause most patients don't know about: Medium-to-deep chemical peels and aggressive laser treatments are best scheduled outside peak summer months (March–June) in Pune. The elevated UV exposure during recovery significantly increases the risk of post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation on Indian skin. If you're planning a peel or laser, aim for July–October, or consult your dermatologist to time it safely.

What She Did Differently

We rebuilt her routine from the ground up for summer. Out went the heavy night cream, the nightly retinol, the thick barrier cream she'd been using since winter. In came a matte SPF 50 reapplied at noon, a niacinamide serum, a stabilised vitamin C every morning, and a prescription depigmenting topical for the melasma that had already deepened.

Six weeks later, she came back. The melasma hadn't spread. The T-zone had calmed noticeably by midday rather than by 9am. The jaw breakouts had cleared. She looked — and this is the word she used — like herself again.

Nothing dramatic. No peels, no lasers. Just the right products, in the right order, calibrated for what this city actually does to skin.

Pune's summer is not the enemy. An uninformed routine is.

Summer Skincare FAQ for Pune

What SPF should I use in Pune summer?

SPF 50 minimum, with PA+++ UVA protection. Apply generously — a ¼ teaspoon for the face — and reapply every 2–3 hours if outdoors. For oily or acne-prone skin, choose a matte or gel formulation. Tinted sunscreens also offer light coverage without the need for additional foundation in summer heat.

How do I prevent skin darkening in summer?

Consistent SPF 50+ is the foundation. Add a stabilised Vitamin C serum in the morning — it neutralises UV-triggered free radicals and suppresses melanin production. Niacinamide in the evening helps fade existing pigmentation. Avoid peak UV hours and use physical barriers (hats, clothing) when outdoors for extended periods.

My skin is very oily in summer — what should I do?

Switch to a gel cleanser, lightweight gel moisturiser, and matte sunscreen. Add niacinamide at 4–5% to regulate sebum production. Avoid skipping moisturiser — dehydrated skin compensates by producing more oil. Do not over-cleanse — it strips the barrier and triggers rebound oiliness.

Can I do skin treatments like peels in summer?

Superficial peels can be done with strict sun protection year-round. Medium to deep peels are better scheduled from July–October in Pune to avoid the high UV exposure during the March–June peak, which significantly increases post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation risk on Indian skin.
